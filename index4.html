07 / Questions

Curious? Start anywhere.

Is ARBITER another autonomous drone?

No. It is the intelligence-and-trust layer around autonomous machines, starting with drones as the first application.

What makes the architecture different?

An independent action-verification boundary between AI autonomy and physical actuation, with a roadmap toward reusable hardware and semiconductor IP.

Does it replace the AI or the flight controller?

No. The AI still plans and the flight controller still flies. ARBITER sits between them and decides whether a proposed action is allowed, bounded or replaced.

What happens if the AI proposes something unsafe?

The proposal is checked against explicit constraints before it reaches the machine. It can be blocked or modified, a safe fallback can take over, and the decision is recorded.

Who is it for?

Teams that operate or build autonomous machines — infrastructure inspection, industrial sites, emergency response and robotics — and researchers working on trustworthy autonomy.
